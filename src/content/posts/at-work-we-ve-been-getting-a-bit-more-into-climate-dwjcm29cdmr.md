At work we've been getting a bit more into climate risk assessments for big infra projects, interrogating the eurocodes (design standards) that are supposedly climate resilient (against, heat, wind loading, intense precipitation expected due to climate change), but are frequently informed by out of date climate projections and restricted in their scope. This leaves no room for failure in evitable large and frequent storms, hot-cool cycles etc that are underestimated within these design specifications, putting infrastructure at risk of additional carbon intensive replacement assets and financial risk during the operational lifespan.

It's made me realise to stop taking design standards for granted (we're an enviro consultancy but also an engineering firm and I had the idea it was the be all and end all). With appropriate knowledge, we can interrogate these, but also note that climate resilience and adaptation has always been found at a community level, and is often regionally and culturally appropriate. Climate resilience measures often springs to mind the ideas of retention ponds and a bitta extra vegetation (again, not specified if longitudinally appropriate to adjust with polar migration caused by climate change), but can be also lively, low maintenance, and creative. Cue, the patios of Córdoba ❤️🤍
