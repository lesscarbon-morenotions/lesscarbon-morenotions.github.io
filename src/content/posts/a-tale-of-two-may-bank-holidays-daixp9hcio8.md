A tale of two May bank holidays 🪸🏖️

For the first heatwave of the year, dystopian as it is, we headed to Margate. Relaxed bohemian vibes, wonky clock tower, the gorgeous shell grotto (which whilst discoloured by the Victorian gas lamps and partially destroyed in WW2) is a site of heritage conservation (& the shells they sell in the shop are sourced sustainably from the Philippines, and sandy beaches peppered with bright parasols and towels.

There's a lot of wall art and signs/bins decorated by local artists and school children to advocate for not littering, coupled with a sense of pride for the area. Even by the beach, there's a bag station to do your own litter pick up (I realised this only after picking up a number of plastic pieces) to put in the bin.

When the framework is there, there is still so much litter in Margate. I saw more plastic than sea lettuce, and unfortunately it was families and groups of <40 yrs most likely to toss items.

Even though littering is becoming more of a taboo topic since Greenpeace and other organisations have highlighted the myriad of long term issues on ecosystems and their inhabitants, bottom up and top down actions need to function in touristic places such of this:

♻️Extended Producer Responsibility in the food sector: fininancially incentivise "bring your own" to foodie stalls and takeaway shops, and take-back schemes 

♻️ Assist corner shops with not selling plastic toys or single use items e.g. 🎈 (now disposable vapes are out, driven by policy!🥳)

♻️ Accessible signs: Large fonts, images, and minimal text specific to plastic

♻️ Investing in bioplastics in the interim

♻️Community clean ups, and corporate social value days for such activities 

♻️ Harsher penalities, and legal implementation (the UK's litter strategy was very light touch and updated in 2017, despite being the main public intersection with waste)

That being said, to be environmental stewards, observation and appreciation of non-human beings and habitats can't necessarily be taught or mandated. Nature-based education is of growing interest and implementation, but will take a generation or two before realised.
