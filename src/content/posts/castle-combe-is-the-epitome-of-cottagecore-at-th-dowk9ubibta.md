Castle Combe is the epitome of cottagecore 🎀 At the base of the Cotswolds, with delightful names like Unicorn Cottage and a picnic service, medieval abodes, and smoke wisping over the river. 🦄🌻It is also, with 150k visitors a year, popular like the much coveted trend, cottagecore (5.3 billion hashtags in 2021). 💐

As popular and aesthetic as the trend is, it has long been documented to have its issues. Romanticising and condensing rural life into duck ponds, home baking and milkmaid dresses, it makes a pantomime of rural life and natural connection🧺 🪶This has been exacerbated by trad wives who paint an unrealistic countryside picture of wealth and carefreeness. Growing up in an isolated corner of North Yorkshire, I would probably be heavily amused by my new propensity to dress up à la princessa and frolick around the fancier, southern version of villages I was once so eager to escape🪺✨, in an area where one of the main reasons for getting a job was to be able to afford a car to actually do stuff, and where sourdough sounded like a bougie urban concept 🍞🥣

@queerbrownvegan is an advocate of gremlincore, a nouveau form of goblincore🍂, which is a less explored counter aesthetic, hinged on maximising use of less glamorous objects, upcycling. It finds beauty in the undesirable, challenging capitalism and colonialism with craft of a subtler kind. 🧶 Whilst celebrating ecosystems and their residents (namely less charismatic ones such as soil systems and bryophytes)🍄‍🟫🦠, it also celebrates human intersections with them, which is mostly expressed in Indigenous practices. In England where first the Celts and then our wise women were murdered long ago, this can pop up in the whispers of herbalism, and the local geoheritage of old masonry 🌳⚒️

But not just the 'core' trends, the supernatural and escapism have long captured the imaginations of conservationists and rural life. By reimagining places from a "fairy ecology" viewpoint or a magic-infused ecocentric approach, can we better connect to our immediate surroundings? ✨🐛
