I love a good zero waste shop, and wanted to make a wee resource for anyone living in or visiting the three places I've lived in my life. Whilst I find a lot of people can dismiss refill or zero waste shops for being too expensive, it's highly probable for more expensive ingredients to get them cheaper in a refill shop — such as grains, or if there's something you don't want to use much of, like a specific salt (the Good Neighbour did a very good post on this in 2020). Also, more money spent locally. The shops also provide paper bags so if you forget your jars it's not an issue.

## Bristol

In Bristol, unsurprisingly, there are many — so I've kept to strictly Bristol City. I've actually only been to Preserve, but possibly only because I am also a huge fan of their desiccated kiwis, so it pulls me back each time.

I have yet to use Oat Float in Bristol since I'm just outside the catchment area, but they're such a cool business that make refilling more accessible by coming to you!

## Dublin

My first refill adventures were in the Source Bulk Food Rathmines during 2020/2021, and as I became a member (many but not all do this), I saved a lot of money. 5% of my previous purchase was applied to the following shop, so I saved so much. One time I even got 5 items for 22c! The cooking oil refill station was also fabulous, and I think cost the same if not less than buying from the supermarket.

## Yorkshire & the North

Back up north, refill shops are harder to come by but I was surprised to see one pop up in Ripon. The Green House and the Rediscovery Centre in Dublin both are predominantly toiletries/laundry focussed to mitigate bathroom waste, so it's good to see some diversity.

Although supermarkets are not there yet — or are in the trial phase of refill stations — Aldi is trialling one in Solihull, and Asda has stations in Leeds, York, Milton Keynes, and Toryglen.

## Why it sticks

In the last 4 years I've found that I mainly shop refill when there's one nearby, which is why we need even more, and integration into more supermarkets.

And on a final note: stay hydrated — and those of us lucky to be in a country with good water infrastructure, don't forget your water bottle.
